The first thing a launch template has to do is look like a product, not a component gallery. We rebuilt the waitlist band around that constraint: one conversion path above the fold, a fuller form at the bottom, and a success state you can demo without standing up Mailchimp on day one.

## Why the waitlist comes first

Most “coming soon” pages treat email capture as an afterthought—a single input lost under a hero illustration. Soonex inverts that. The hero carries a compact email field so visitors who are ready can convert immediately. The waitlist section below adds context: role, interests, team size, and a short note field. Together they read like a product signup, not a generic newsletter block.

That split matters for founders who need to show investors or early customers a credible funnel before backend work lands. You can walk someone through the page, submit the form, and show the toast without apologising for placeholder behaviour.

## What shipped in this cut

The forms use **named fields** throughout. That is deliberate: when you wire a provider, you should not rename inputs or restructure markup.

| Field | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| `waitlist[email]` | Primary identifier |
| `waitlist[role]` | Founder, engineer, designer, etc. |
| `waitlist[interests]` | Multi-select tags for segmentation |
| `waitlist[team_size]` | Company stage signal |
| `waitlist[notes]` | Free-text launch context |

Submit handlers in this demo only trigger a Corex toast. No addresses are stored server-side. The copy in the toast is easy to replace when you connect ConvertKit, Buttondown, or your own endpoint.

### Hero vs full band

- **Hero form** — email plus a single Join button. Keeps the fold tight on mobile.
- **Waitlist band** — full field set inside a framed panel. Skins restyle the panel without touching field names.
- **Toast** — confirms success with title and description slots you control in HEEx.

## Wiring a real list later

When you are ready to collect addresses, keep the markup and point the form action at your provider or a small Phoenix/LiveView handler. The template already validates email client-side; add server-side validation and rate limiting in production.

1. Choose a list provider or build an internal endpoint.
2. Replace the demo submit handler with a POST to that endpoint.
3. Update the privacy page to describe what you store and for how long.
4. Turn on analytics only after updating cookie consent if you measure conversions.

> The waitlist is the conversion. Everything else on the page exists to make that click feel finished.

> [!NOTE]
> This demo does not collect addresses. The same form structure is what you ship when your list is live.

## Accessibility and motion

Both forms use Corex native inputs and selects with visible labels or screen-reader-only labels where space is tight. Reduced motion does not remove the forms; it only disables ornamental scroll reveals. Test keyboard flow from the hero through the waitlist band before you swap photography.

## What we did not add

We intentionally skipped double opt-in UI, referral codes, and social login. Those belong in your product layer once you know your compliance requirements. The template gives you a professional shell; you add policy and persistence.

If you fork Soonex today, replace the hero still, rewrite the toast copy, and you already have a waitlist story worth showing—not a lorem block waiting for design polish.
